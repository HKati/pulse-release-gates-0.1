# PULSEmech / Paradoxon Diagram

## Relations, Conditions and Compatible Change

**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**Standing:** conceptual research draft with hand-worked illustrations  
**Scope:** independent development of the workshop question; documentation only  
**Authority effect:** none

## 1. Starting from existing results

The workshop direction is expressed by **“ABBÓL” — “FROM THAT.”** Available
results become the starting material for an inquiry into the relations that
hold, the conditions under which they hold, and the variations with which they
remain compatible. [W1]

An accumulated collection may contain individually correct results while
leaving their joint possibilities unresolved. The values alone may not settle
which results belong to the same configuration, which conditions can hold
together, what must vary together, or which difference would affect another
part of the examined structure.

The governing question is:

> Starting from what is available, which relations can be established, under
> which conditions do they hold, and what do those conditions permit us to
> conclude about compatible variations and their impact?

This question can require revisiting the distinctions used to describe the
results themselves. Discovering a relevant relation may reveal that two things
previously given the same label must be distinguished, or that several separate
records concern one jointly defined configuration. The initial description is
therefore open to refinement.

The phrase **linear accumulation** is used here for presenting results one
after another without preserving or examining the relations needed for the
question being asked. It is a workshop term, not a definition of mathematical
linearity. A sequence can carry adequate relational information when its
bindings and conditions are retained. A numerical report can also express a
joint property. The issue is what the description preserves and supports.

### 1.1 Quantity and recurrence

The later workshop contribution asks whether a growing quantity can conceal
recurrence in the organization producing or relating the results. [W2]

A counter may keep increasing while a relevant relation, condition or manner
of producing a result recurs. The complete states can differ numerically while
a specifically identified organization returns. The examination must therefore
state what repeats, which distinctions are retained, and why any ignored
coordinate does not alter the claim being made.

An increasing quantity alone establishes neither structural development nor a
cycle. For example, an unbounded sequence of distinct counter values has no
exact repeated counter state. Whether a relevant relational configuration
recurs is a further question. A cycle drawn after discarding a resource limit
or a subject identity may have no corresponding repeatable operation.

The inquiry asks whether additional results disclose a relevant relational
difference, provide new support, leave existing possibilities unchanged, or
make a previously encountered configuration possible again. The answer may
also remain unresolved. This preserves the workshop question without making
cyclicity a compulsory explanation of every pattern or increase.

### 1.2 A computed value and an established operation

A numerical result can be a carrier of relational information when its meaning
and bindings are preserved. The inquiry concerns the relation or operation it
supports, including what was discarded in producing that representation.

Calculation, a mathematical proof under stated premises, and establishment of
a particular operation are different claims. A proof requires attention to
its statement and assumptions; applying it to a concrete subject additionally
requires the relevant correspondence and conditions. [E1]

The quoted commentary accompanying the workshop question contains broader
claims about mathematics, all patterns and all bots. Those generalizations are
not premises of this draft. Formal expressibility does not guarantee a general
computational method. [E2] A case must establish its own pattern, recurrence
and operational meaning. The word **movement**, where used for an actual
operation, requires the corresponding participants, conditions and consequences
to be specified; a larger count alone does not establish them.

## 2. Independence of the inquiry

The concepts are developed from the question above and the distinctions needed
to examine it. An existing formalism may later provide useful notation or an
instrument for a particular part. Its suitability depends on what it preserves
when applied to that part.

No particular representation is selected as the complete form of the inquiry.
In particular, a list of pairwise edges, a global sequence of events, or a
decision procedure is not assumed to exhaust the relations under examination.
The number and nature of the participants, the kinds of conditions involved,
and the required distinctions remain questions to be specified in each case.

A later translation into a chosen formalism must make its scope explicit. It
must identify which distinctions are carried across, which require additional
structure, and which remain unrepresented. A case that does not fit remains a
limit or an extension question; its meaning must not be silently changed to
make it fit.

Comparisons with other approaches can be made after this independent
description is sufficiently clear. Similar terminology alone establishes
neither equivalence nor a difference. The comparison must concern the meaning,
conditions and consequences actually retained.

## 3. What is given and what is still to be established

An inquiry begins with a description of what is available: results, records,
observations, statements, distinctions, or other identified material. Their
scope and meaning matter. Two equal values may refer to different subjects or
different conditions; two differently written records may concern the same
subject.

The initial account should make clear:

- what each available item concerns;
- which identities and meanings are fixed for the examination;
- which bindings or joint conditions are already supported;
- which relations are proposed for investigation;
- which relevant parts remain unknown or outside the selected scope.

These are questions for describing a case, not a fixed data schema. The inquiry
may require distinctions that the initial account does not yet contain.

The presence of a record, the validity of its content under stated conditions,
and the relation of that content to another item are separate matters. A
relation may be present in the available material without having been made
explicit. It may instead require additional observation or a new justified
derivation. Those situations must remain distinguishable.

Further examination of the same material can expose consequences that were
previously unnoticed. It cannot by itself supply a missing premise as an
established fact. When several structures remain compatible with what is
available, their multiplicity is part of the result.

## 4. The holding of a relation

Here, **relation** is a working term for how specified participants or
distinctions stand together under stated conditions. A concrete use must say
what is being related and what it means for that relation to hold. A name or a
drawn connection does not supply that meaning.

Participants can include several elements together, a configuration, or
relations already under examination. A condition can concern their joint
arrangement. Pairwise representation is one possible instrument when it
preserves the required joint distinctions.

For a proposed relation, the inquiry asks:

1. What exactly does the relation concern?
2. What does its holding assert within the selected scope?
3. Which conditions are required, and which are sufficient if that has been
   established?
4. What supports the assertion that those conditions are met?
5. What would distinguish its holding from its failure or from an unresolved
   case?

A recorded association may justify a narrow relational statement. A stronger
claim about dependence, persistence or an actual effect needs the conditions
appropriate to that stronger meaning. The meaning must remain visible when a
relation is reused in another part of the inquiry.

Conditions can themselves involve other relations. They may have to be
examined jointly. This does not require selecting an event order before their
joint compatibility can be considered.

## 5. Preservation and compatible variation

**Preservation** asks whether a specified relation or condition continues to
hold across identified alternatives. The comparison must state what may vary,
what is held fixed, and how identities and meanings correspond between the
alternatives.

A relation can be preserved while individual values change. Conversely,
individual values can remain equal while their joint bindings or conditions
change. Both possibilities matter to an examination starting from results.

**Compatibility** concerns what can hold together under the stated conditions.
It can apply to values, relations, conditions, or proposed configurations. It
does not imply an equal weight for the alternatives, a preferred alternative,
or permission to carry out an operation.

Several questions follow:

- Which variations preserve the relation under examination?
- Which participants or conditions must vary together?
- Which combinations become excluded or newly possible?
- Which distinctions remain unresolved by the available description?
- Does the proposed variation alter the meaning of a condition as well as its
  satisfaction?

The last question prevents a change of meaning from appearing to be ordinary
preservation. If two variants use a condition differently, a correspondence
between the meanings must be established before a precise preservation claim
is made.

The applicable conditions may also depend on the configuration. A condition
can retain its meaning while becoming applicable to a different participant
or configuration. This differs both from a change in satisfaction for the
same application and from a change in the meaning of the condition itself.
These distinctions belong in the comparison when they matter to the relation
under examination.

Comparison across alternatives does not establish that an actual transition
between them occurred. An application concerning persistence through an actual
change must also establish the relevant occurrence and connection. A temporal
or causal interpretation requires its own justification.

## 6. What an impact claim means

An **impact** claim must identify the change considered and the distinction
affected by it. In a structural examination, this may be a change in joint
possibilities, the satisfaction of a condition, or the compatibility of other
relations under the stated assumptions.

An impact can involve several relations together. A chain drawn between items
does not by itself establish how a change is transmitted or whether its stated
consequence follows. The relation meanings, relevant conditions and any
alternative support must be retained in the examination.

Three subjects of change need to remain distinct:

| Subject of change | Question being examined |
|---|---|
| The examined structure | Has a relation, participant or condition changed in the case itself? |
| Our knowledge of that structure | Has new information established, contradicted or left unresolved a relation? |
| The representation used | Has a distinction or binding been added, removed or expressed differently in the description? |

These subjects can interact. They are not assumed to be independent physical
layers. The distinction identifies what a particular claim actually concerns.

Removing a record from a description need not remove the corresponding
relation from the examined system. Discovering a relation need not create that
relation in the system. Equally, a system can change before the available
description reveals the change.

Where the distinction cannot yet be resolved, the claim must retain that
uncertainty. An observed change in reporting supports a reporting-change claim;
an assertion about the underlying structure requires the additional connection
appropriate to that assertion.

### 6.1 Return, recurrence and renewed failure possibility

Three claims can arise when a modification is examined:

| Claim | What has to be established |
|---|---|
| Exact return | The specified complete state returns under the stated identity rules. |
| Structural recurrence | The relevant relations and conditions recur under a justified correspondence, although other values may differ. |
| Renewed failure possibility | The same precisely identified failure condition becomes possible again, possibly through a different arrangement. |

Renewed failure possibility can exist without a cycle. Structural recurrence
can also be harmless. A recorded error appearing twice does not by itself
identify the intervening relations or the mechanism of return.

For an operational recurrence claim, the comparison must preserve the
conditions enabling continuation. Counters, remaining resources, permissions,
identities or accumulated effects cannot be omitted when they change what can
happen next. A return in an abbreviated description can be only a candidate
until a compatible course in the full declared case is established.

### 6.2 The repair question

For a proposed repair, keep the meaning of the target failure fixed and ask:

1. Which relations or conditions cease to hold?
2. Which remain, including alternative paths or supporting conditions?
3. Which new relations or combinations become possible?
4. Can the resulting structure permit the previous failure again, including
   through a different route?

A local condition holding immediately after a repair does not establish that
all relevant permitted continuations preserve it. Conversely, a chain in a
representation does not establish that its steps can be combined for one
subject under jointly compatible conditions.

The next research unit develops these questions in
[Cyclic Impact of Repairs](PULSEMECH_PARADOX_CYCLIC_IMPACT_RESEARCH_v0_1.md).
It remains within the Paradoxon Diagram and Transition Meter inquiry. The
initial task is to identify possible courses from declared relations and
conditions, with missing support retained. It does not claim to predict the
next actual incident.

## 7. Multiplicity, missing support and contradiction

Several structures may each be compatible with the available material while
remaining mutually incompatible as descriptions of a single case. An
unresolved account must retain the alternatives relevant to the question
instead of selecting one because it is familiar, convenient or narratively
complete.

The following situations carry different meanings:

| Situation | Meaning within the examination |
|---|---|
| A relation is established within scope | The stated support is sufficient for the specified relational claim under its conditions. |
| A relation is proposed | Its holding is under investigation. |
| Support is missing | The available material does not settle the specified claim. |
| A relation is ruled out within scope | Its holding conflicts with an established condition of the examined case. |
| The description is inconsistent | The selected statements cannot all hold together under their declared meanings. |
| The case has not been examined | No conclusion about that case has yet been obtained. |

This table separates meanings; it does not define an implementation status
enumeration. Where records are later designed, origin, justification and a
separate operational decision must not be collapsed into one label.

Missing support does not establish that a relation is absent. An inconsistent
description does not establish a universal impossibility beyond its scope. The
loss of one supporting record need not remove another valid basis for the same
claim.

**Preserving unresolvedness is a methodological requirement.** It protects the
inquiry from introducing a relation that the available material does not
establish. It is not, by itself, a hypothesis about a physical mechanism or a
claim that an implemented system already satisfies this requirement.

## 8. Hand-worked illustrations

The following small cases make selected distinctions explicit. Their finite
domains and conditions are choices for these illustrations. They do not define
the general form of the Paradoxon Diagram or establish coverage of an actual
system.

Sections 8.1 and 8.3 restate bounded examples from the cited repository
documents; Sections 8.2 and 8.4 develop those cases for the present explanation.

### 8.1 Equal individual possibilities, different joint possibilities

Consider two identified positions, `A` and `B`, each taking a value in `{0,1}`.
Two descriptions specify the complete joint possibilities as follows:

| Description | Permitted joint assignments `(A,B)` | Individual possibilities for `A` | Individual possibilities for `B` |
|---|---|---|---|
| U | `(0,0)`, `(1,1)` | `{0,1}` | `{0,1}` |
| V | `(0,1)`, `(1,0)` | `{0,1}` | `{0,1}` |

The individual possibilities are identical. The question “can both positions
have value 1 together?” has different answers: yes in U, no in V.

If only the individual possibilities are available, they do not determine
which of these joint descriptions holds. Recovering the distinction requires
support for the joint condition. Repeating the individual value lists does not
resolve this particular ambiguity.

This is also the bounded distinction exhibited by the T1 example in the M1–M3
definition contract. [R1, Section 4.2]

### 8.2 Preservation can require joint variation

Retain description U and its condition that `A` and `B` have equal values.
Starting from the named assignment `(0,0)`, compare two proposed alternatives:

| Proposed alternative | Condition of equality preserved? |
|---|---|
| Change only `A`, giving `(1,0)` | No |
| Change both values, giving `(1,1)` | Yes |

For this fully specified case, the relation is preserved by a joint variation
while the individual values change. Fixing every individual value is therefore
not necessary for preserving this relation.

The comparison says which alternatives satisfy the condition. It does not say
that either change occurred, identify a physical mechanism for it, or establish
which participant would cause a change in the other.

### 8.3 Pairwise summaries can omit a joint distinction

Consider three binary positions and two complete collections of jointly bound
records. Each row occurs once:

| Row | Collection E | Collection O |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `000` | `001` |
| 2 | `011` | `010` |
| 3 | `101` | `100` |
| 4 | `110` | `111` |

For any selected pair of positions, both collections have the same counts:
`00`, `01`, `10` and `11` each appear once. Nevertheless, every row in E has an
even number of ones and every row in O has an odd number.

The pairwise counts therefore do not determine this joint distinction. The
example concerns summaries that discard the shared row binding. Pair records
that retain enough common identifiers may preserve the joint information and
must be assessed accordingly. [R2, Section 3]

This illustrates why the inquiry must allow a relation to concern several
participants together. It does not require any particular drawing or storage
format, and it does not make every pairwise representation inadequate.

### 8.4 Removing support and changing a relation

Suppose a retained record establishes which complete joint description, U or
V, applies to a selected case. Removing that record can leave the examiner
unable to decide between U and V. No change in the underlying case follows
merely from the record's removal.

Separately, suppose the examined structure actually changes from the condition
specified by U to that specified by V. The joint possibilities then differ.
Establishing that actual change requires support for the structural claim; the
absence of a record cannot substitute for it.

If an independent retained basis still establishes U, loss of the first record
need not even remove the examiner's ability to identify the joint description.
The effect of losing support depends on the support that remains.

## 9. What the inquiry proposes

The research proposal is that examining relations, their conditions and
compatible variations can disclose distinctions left unresolved when existing
results are considered only through an insufficient description of how they
stand together.

The hand-worked cases establish specific distinctions under their stated
conditions. A broader method still needs a defined scope, a way to obtain or
justify the relevant relational information, and an account of the distinctions
its representation preserves.

The open work includes:

1. **Meaning of holding.** Specify concrete relational claims without silently
   strengthening an association into another kind of relation.
2. **Participant refinement.** Determine when the available description must be
   refined to express a distinction discovered during the inquiry.
3. **Conditions of preservation.** Identify which conditions are necessary,
   sufficient, alternative, or still unknown in a particular case.
4. **Joint and mutual variation.** Examine cases whose conditions must be
   considered together, without imposing a prior event sequence.
5. **Impact scope.** State which differences the examination can reveal and
   which remain outside its coverage.
6. **Representation adequacy.** Show that the selected representation retains
   the distinctions required for the claim made with it.

An informative case can expose a missing condition or a limit of a proposed
description. Such a result remains part of the inquiry even when it provides
no unique answer to the original question.

## 10. Conditions for a later comparative study

A comparative study becomes appropriate when a specific claim of benefit has
been formulated. Its design should follow that claim rather than defining the
research direction in advance.

Two different comparisons must be distinguished:

| Comparison | What is held constant | What a result can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Different treatment of the same available information | Complete input information, meanings, identities and question | A scoped difference in what the examined procedures preserve, expose or correctly determine |
| Addition or removal of relational information | Case, question and the unchanged parts of the method | The contribution or limitation of the changed information or binding within that case |

If one side receives additional evidence, its advantage cannot be attributed
solely to a different representation of identical information. The addition
itself can still be studied as an explicit change.

Expected distinctions and counterexamples should be specified independently of
the produced conclusion. The study should include unresolved cases, redundant
support, cases where both approaches succeed, and cases where the proposed
representation loses something relevant. A claimed benefit must remain within
what those comparisons actually establish.

These are conditions for a future comparison. No benchmark result, universal
advantage or general completeness claim is supplied by this draft.

## 11. Relationship to the existing Paradoxon work

The M1–M3 definition contract already develops a bounded candidate treatment of
joint possibilities, persistence conditions and evidence applicability. It
explicitly separates its selected finite profile from complete coverage of the
Paradoxon mechanics or an actual system. Its notation can support a specified
part of the present inquiry when the mapping preserves the relevant meanings.
[R1, Sections 1–5 and 6.1–6.3]

The relation-preserving discrimination note provides a further bounded example
in which equal local and pairwise summaries leave a declared joint distinction
unresolved. It also distinguishes removal of an observation binding from a
change in the observed source. [R2, Sections 3–4]

These connections preserve continuity with the existing research while leaving
the present question open to relations and conditions beyond the selected
examples. Their formal instruments are scoped contributions; they do not fix
the complete structure of the inquiry.

The Workshop accumulation note separately distinguishes quantitative growth,
representational changes and opening a scoped operational capability. Its
specific operational test is a related instrument with its own assumptions;
it does not settle recurrence in the present inquiry. [R3]

Any later application to a PULSEmech execution or decision path requires a
separate mapping of the particular claim, its supporting material and the
requirements of that path. This conceptual draft introduces no gate, execution
permission or release-authority change. Its illustrations are not evidence of
a newly executed experiment.
